# Grounding for AGENTS.md

*Each entry anchors a named rule; `[against]` marks evidence that cuts against the rule it informs and is kept for that reason. Quotations were checked against the source on 2026-09-28; `(secondary)` marks those seen only in a secondary source. Preprints are marked as such.*

- **Popper: falsification, and who sets the criteria** ([falsify], [provisional]). "Every genuine test of a theory is an attempt to falsify it, or to refute it." "The more a theory forbids, the better it is." (*Conjectures and Refutations*, 1963, ch. 1, theses 3 and 5.) "… criteria of refutation have to be laid down beforehand: it must be agreed which observable situations, if actually observed, mean that the theory is refuted." (Ibid., ch. 1, n. 3, aimed at theories that read every observation as support.) "Methodological rules are here regarded as conventions. They might be described as the rules of the game of empirical science"; the supreme rule says the others "must be designed in such a way that they do not protect any statement in science against falsification." (*The Logic of Scientific Discovery*, 1959, §11.) Test results are accepted "by agreement", like a jury verdict that may be "quashed or revised" (§30).
- **Duhem, Quine: the conjunction** ([tiers]). "An Experiment in Physics Can Never Condemn an Isolated Hypothesis but Only a Whole Theoretical Group" (*The Aim and Structure of Physical Theory*, Eng. 1954, Part II, ch. VI, §2 heading). "Our statements about the external world face the tribunal of sense experience not individually but only as a corporate body." ("Two Dogmas of Empiricism", 1951, §5.)
- **Lakatos, Kuhn, Putnam: why blame-order is a heuristic** ([tiers], [provisional]) `[against]` naive falsification. Kuhn: "either no scientific theory ever confronts a counterinstance, or all such theories confront counterinstances at all times" (*Structure of Scientific Revolutions*, ch. VIII). Lakatos (1970) denies crucial tests; Putnam (1974): practitioners first blame auxiliary hypotheses. Hence a failing check prompts re-derivation, and one project's failure revises a rule through a jury, not by reflex.
- **Miller (1974), Tichý (1974): the method applied to its author** ([provisional]). They independently showed Popper's definition of verisimilitude can compare theories only when those theories are true; Popper accepted the refutation.
- **Dijkstra, Feynman: agreement is not truth** ([tiers], [falsify]). "Program testing can be used to show the presence of bugs, but never to show their absence!" (secondary). "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool." (Feynman, "Cargo Cult Science", 1974.)
- **Knight & Leveson; Ron, Baudry & Monperrus: independence is measured, not assumed** ([tiers]) `[against]` an unconditional tier (a). Independently written versions of one spec failed together far more than chance predicts (IEEE TSE 12(1), 1986); with coding agents: "substantial common-mode failure, along the findings of Knight-Leveson" (arXiv:2606.20158, preprint, 2026).
- **Gloaguen et al.: redundant maps cost, gap-filling maps help** ([maps]). Context files raised inference cost 20–23% with no significant change in task success; "repository overviews, although popular and recommended by model providers, are not helpful"; and `[against]` a blanket ban: "context files can act as effective overviews when no documentation is present" (App. B). (arXiv:2602.11988, 2026.)
- **Parnas & Clements; Naur: what code cannot hold** ([maps], [residue], [mismatch]). The module guide records each module's hidden design decision ("A Rational Design Process: How and Why to Fake It", 1986), and the same paper names the failure [residue] bans: "Boring prose. Lots of words are used to say what could be said by a single programming language statement." Naur: "program revival … merely from the documentation, is strictly impossible" ("Programming as Theory Building", 1985).
- **Ousterhout: interface comments** ([residue]). "Without comments there is no way to have abstraction or modularity"; interface contracts are what a name cannot carry (*A Philosophy of Software Design*; aposd-vs-clean-code).
- **Radmanesh et al.; Chakrabarti: prose drifts, and only its why makes deletion safe** ([residue], [prose], [working-memory]). Code–comment inconsistency: "around 1.5 times more likely to lead to a bug-introducing commit" (arXiv:2409.10781, 2024). Agent instruction files "grow without bound … more than tripling over their lifetime (+226%)"; comments encoding the latent reasoning removed 99.3% of the excess instructions (arXiv:2608.11095, preprint, 2026).
- **Context cost** (receiver model, [prose]). Mid-context information is used worst (Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle", arXiv:2307.03172). "Model performance is dramatically decreased when irrelevant information is included" (Shi et al., arXiv:2302.00093). Across 18 models, "performance varies significantly as input length changes, even on simple tasks" (Chroma, "Context Rot", 2025, technical report).
- **In-context steering** (receiver model). In-context learning occurs "when the LM also infers a shared latent concept between examples in a prompt" (Xie et al., arXiv:2111.02080). "Randomly replacing labels in the demonstrations barely hurts performance"; the drivers are label space, input distribution and "the overall format of the sequence" (Min et al., arXiv:2202.12837).
- **Fan and collaborators: receiver-shaped representation** (receiver model, [maps], [names], [prose]). Sketchers produce "detailed drawings when necessary, but simpler drawings when sufficient" (Fan, Hawkins, Wu & Goodman, *Comput. Brain Behav.*, 2020). Explanations eased inferring "which action was needed to operate the machine, but [made it] more difficult to identify which machine it represented" (Huey, Oey, Lloyd & Fan, *Cognition*, 2023): a map depicts, a why explains, and each costs the other. Viewers who saw one pair's drawings in sequence improved +15.8% vs +5.6% for a shuffled mix, and drawings "did not drift toward 'arbitrariness'" (Hawkins, Sano, Goodman & Fan, *Nat. Commun.* 14:2199, 2023). Sparser sketches "exhibited greater semantic ambiguity" for humans and vision models alike (Mukherjee et al., NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks, 2023). Human and vision-model subjects only.
